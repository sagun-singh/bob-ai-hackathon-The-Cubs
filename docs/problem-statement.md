# Problem Statement

## Background

[Law enforcement agencies generate large volumes of FIR data containing valuable information about crimes, victims, locations, accused descriptions, and modus operandi (MO). However, much of this information is stored as unstructured text, making it difficult to quickly analyze and connect related cases.]

## The Problem

[Investigators often have to manually read and cross-reference multiple FIRs to identify similar crimes and repeat-offender patterns. Differences in names, dates, locations, and wording can make it difficult to recognize that different FIRs may share a similar modus operandi.]


## Who is Affected

[Local police station officers, crime branch analysts, and investigators who need to examine large numbers of FIRs and identify connections between cases are directly affected by this problem.]

## Why It Matters

[Manual analysis takes valuable investigation time and may cause important connections between cases to be overlooked. Faster identification of similar crime patterns can help investigators organize information and focus their attention on potentially related cases.]

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

[Traditional FIR systems primarily store and retrieve case information but do not automatically understand the meaning of free-flowing FIR text or compare the modus operandi across multiple cases. Pattern identification often depends on manual investigation and cross-referencing.]
